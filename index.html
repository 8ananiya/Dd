How this works & what it can't do

The signal chain

  • Transparency mode — your mic is piped straight to your headphones through a high-pass / low-pass / presence EQ. Zero added latency, so it sounds natural.
  • ANC modes — the signal runs through an AudioWorklet that performs zero-phase spectral subtraction: a 512-point FFT, a per-bin noise-floor estimate (fast-attack / slow-release minimum tracker), a Wiener-style gain curve with over-subtraction, and frequency + time smoothing to kill "musical noise". Adds ~10.6 ms of latency — inaudible for noise reduction.
  • Voice Focus — heavy low-frequency rejection plus a +6 dB presence lift at 2.5 kHz so speech cuts through traffic or machinery.

Honest limitations

  • This is not true phase-inverting ANC. Real ANC needs a reference mic outside the ear cup and an error mic inside it, plus sub-millisecond DSP on a real-time thread. A single mic can't do feed-forward cancellation.
  • It cannot inject audio into a phone call. Browsers have no API to replace the mic stream an ongoing call is already using.
  • Because the mic hears your headphones, feedback is a real risk. The built-in limiter catches runaway gain, but it isn't a substitute for caution.
  • Everything here runs locally in your browser. No audio ever leaves your device.

For real ANC you'd need a native app (Android AudioRecord/AudioTrack, iOS AVAudioEngine) with an NLMS/FxLMS adaptive filter running on a low-latency audio thread.
