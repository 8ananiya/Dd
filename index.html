How it works

  • Binaural beats — two pure tones, one per ear, offset by a few Hz. Your brainstem perceives the difference as a rhythmic pulse at that offset frequency.
  • Isochronic pulses — a single tone switched on/off at the target rate. Stronger, more direct entrainment; works without headphones.
  • Carrier tones — Solfeggio-adjacent frequencies (396, 528, 639, 852 Hz) chosen for each state, layered as a soft pad.
  • Ambient noise — pink or brown noise masked gently under the tones for a smoother bed.

Safety

  • Not a medical device. No clinical claim is made.
  • Do not use while driving or operating machinery.
  • Skip if you have epilepsy, a seizure disorder, or a pacemaker — rhythmic audio stimulation is a known trigger for some people.
  • Stop if you feel dizzy, anxious, or headachy. Lower the volume. Binaural beats are best around 40–60 dB, not loud.
  • Binaural beats need headphones; isochronic and pad layers work on speakers.
